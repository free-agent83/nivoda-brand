# Brand Application Rules

## Purpose
Context-specific rules for applying the Nivoda brand. The voice, visual identity, and writing style are defined in their own files — this file governs **how they combine differently depending on where the brand shows up**.

The first question to ask before producing anything: **is this editorial or UI?** These two modes have different rules. Apply the wrong mode and the brand breaks.

---

## The Two Modes

### Editorial mode
Anywhere the brand is performing — expressing identity, telling a story, persuading, presenting. The full brand vocabulary is permitted.

**Applies to:** marketing pages (nivoda.com, blog, campaign landing pages), sales and investor decks, pitch materials, conference handoffs (JCK, partner collateral, event signage), longform documents (whitepapers, reports, brand books), hero moments (splash screens, marketing emails, brand films, social hero assets), internal brand onboarding and all-hands presentations.

**Vocabulary available in editorial mode:**
- Decorative violet eyebrows (section eyebrows with the violet hairline beneath)
- Italic call-and-response (italic violet `<em>` at final beat of Nanum Myeongjo headlines)
- Dual-context palette — warm light (`#f5f0ec`) with white cards, or dark (`#0c0a09`) for immersive moments
- The two-violet rule on dark (violet-500 for mass, violet-400 for thin)
- Promotional banner photography (procession mode)
- Editorial photography (perspective, light, palette)
- Nanum Myeongjo at display scale for headlines
- JetBrains Mono for structural labels and eyebrows

**Restraint still applies:** violet is precious — maximum 2–3 violet roles per composition. The full vocabulary is permitted, not mandatory.

---

### UI mode
Anywhere the brand is serving a task — the Nivoda app, dashboards, transactional flows, marketplace, forms, data tables. The brand recedes; the work comes forward.

**Applies to:** the Nivoda platform (buyer and supplier facing), internal tooling, dashboards, checkout flows, form pages, data tables, transactional email (order confirmation, shipping updates, invoices).

**Rules for UI mode:**
- **Monochrome chrome at rest.** Buttons, navigation, and interactive elements are stone-coloured (dark) or stone-950 (light) at rest. Violet is hidden.
- **Violet revealed only on hover/focus.** Buttons hover to violet-500 (dark) or violet-600 (light). Links hover to violet-600. Inputs get a violet-600 focus ring. Violet signals interaction, not decoration.
- **No decorative violet eyebrows.** Section labels in UI are Inter or JetBrains Mono in stone — never with the decorative violet hairline. That is an editorial gesture.
- **No call-and-response in UI.** No italic violet type. The gesture belongs to display moments, not task surfaces.
- **Photography carries colour.** In UI contexts where imagery appears, it uses the editorial photography principles (warm, human, jeweller's perspective). The image provides colour; the chrome stays neutral.
- **Logo in nav only.** Never repeated within page content.
- **Typography follows the design system scale.** No bespoke sizing; use Clarity V2 tokens.

## Product UI (detailed)

The primary brand surface. Most users encounter Nivoda through the product.

- Brand expression is **restrained**. Professional and functional, not promotional.
- Logo in navigation bar only — never repeated within page content.
- Violet is used sparingly: CTAs on hover, active states, key accent moments. Never as a background fill at rest.
- Typography follows the Clarity V2 design system scale exactly. No bespoke sizing.
- Copy follows the writing style guide. No marketing language in product UI.
- Empty states and illustrations are the main opportunity for brand personality within the product.

**The line:** Product UI is a tool, not a billboard. Users are here to transact. Brand builds trust and clarity — it never competes with the task.

---

## Marketing & Editorial Surfaces (detailed)

Marketing and editorial surfaces are where the full brand vocabulary is deployed.

- Brand expression is **confident and performative**. This is where identity comes through.
- Nanum Myeongjo at display scale for hero headings and section titles.
- Call-and-response (italic violet em at final beat of Nanum headlines) is permitted and encouraged in hero moments.
- Decorative violet eyebrows with hairline beneath: yes.
- Dual-context palette: either warm light (`#f5f0ec`) + white cards, or dark (`#0c0a09`) immersive — choose one per composition, do not mix.
- Photography follows editorial mode rules (perspective, light, palette) — see visual-identity.md.
- Category banners in the marketplace use promotional banner mode (procession) — see visual-identity.md.
- Copy register shifts to warm and persuasive — see voice-and-tone.md.

**The line:** Marketing is a story. Polished, intentional, human. Never generic, never hyperbolic.

---

## Email

- **Promotional/marketing emails:** follow editorial mode rules. The inbox is personal space — slightly reduced intensity but the full vocabulary is permitted. One primary CTA per email.
- **Transactional emails** (order confirmation, shipping updates, invoices): follow UI mode rules. Clear, concise, action-oriented. No decorative violet. No call-and-response. The task is the focus.
- Subject lines: sentence case, no ALL CAPS, no clickbait.
- Every email must be scannable in under 10 seconds.

---

## Documentation & Help

- Voice register: precise, patient, structured. Never casual, never promotional.
- Step-by-step instructions with numbered lists.
- Screenshots must use current UI — never outdated.
- Technical terms defined on first use.
- Write for the least experienced user — never assume expertise.

---

## Internal Communications

- Voice register: casual, direct, no jargon inflation.
- Slack, Notion, internal docs — the brand still applies at lowest formality.
- Never use internal shorthand in any context that might be forwarded externally.

## How to Use This File

**Agents:** Before producing output, identify the mode (editorial or UI) and the specific context. Apply the rules for that mode. If a context is ambiguous or not covered, flag a BRAND GAP — do not improvise.

**Humans:** Use this to review brand application across surfaces. Each mode has a different vocabulary. This file defines which vocabulary is permitted where.
